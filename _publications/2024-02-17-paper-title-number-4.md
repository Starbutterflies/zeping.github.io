Abstract:  On-board diagnostic (OBD) systems with remote transmission capabilities are increasingly used to monitor NOx emissions from heavy-duty diesel vehicles (HDDVs) across China. Yet, the full potential of OBD data remains untapped due to data quality limitations. This study introduces a data-driven method to identify urea depletion segments and calculate urea consumption rates using median filtering and a sliding window approach. Among 328 daily-scale vehicles, 23 (7%) exhibited no detectable urea use, suggesting possible tampering. However, due to mixed vehicle types and short observation periods, this indicator alone is insufficient. To improve reliability, we analyze monthly- and annual-scale data from same-model vehicles and propose a baseline-based detection framework. Results show signs of tampering or abnormal use in 1 of 6 annual-scale and 4 of 22 monthly-scale vehicles. The approach is scalable, cost-effective, and suitable for integration into big-data platforms, offering policymakers a practical tool for targeted enforcement and emission reduction.
